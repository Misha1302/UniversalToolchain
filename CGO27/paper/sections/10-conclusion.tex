\section{Conclusion}
A generic IR can be structurally valid while violating the semantic contracts of the selected compiler. Contract-guided reverification makes this gap explicit: extensions declare fact effects and verifier ownership, and a fail-closed scheduler chooses selective or unconditional discharge. Across a frozen System W boundary corpus, a 30-case source-to-result study, and a public-SDK TensorRules package, selective verification matches always-verify classification on evaluated cases and detects faults missed by structural or invalidation-only policies. Eight isolated mechanism ablations provide necessity witnesses for producer/source identity, order, ownership, conflict, routing, capability, and occurrence checks; policy ablations separately attribute gains to typed contracts and obligation discharge. The evidence supports a correctness and localization contribution, not a speedup or broad external-validity claim. Those claims remain gated on a pinned-machine experiment and an externally authored frozen corpus.
