\section{Conclusion}
Obligation-guided reverification distinguishes three events that ordinary invalidation often conflates: a selected semantic fact becomes invalid, a canonical owner is responsible for re-establishing it, and a particular boundary is the deadline for doing so. The production implementation now retains fact state and pending obligations across bytecode, AIR, optimized-AIR, and backend-input observations, including a route created before it becomes executable. Under explicit sound-seed, sound-and-complete-effect, routing, scheduler-coverage, and verifier-soundness assumptions, P2 fails closed before a due false or undischarged considered fact crosses its first eligible boundary.

The executable demand baseline shows one reason this is stronger than lazy recomputation: P1D protects a queried invalidated fact, but an otherwise identical unqueried fault can cross the semantic boundary. Verifier-after-every-pass is a stronger alternative when one generic verifier expresses all required relations; our distinct contribution is selected-fact ownership, route availability, and deferred fail-closed deadlines across heterogeneous checks. On the versioned boundary, System W source-to-result, and Language T corpora, P2 and P3 agree on every evaluated classification while P2 avoids routes with no due obligation. The result is a bounded enforcement and localization contribution. It is not a proof of supplied contracts, a whole-compiler proof, a universal P2/P3 equivalence result, an externally independent evaluation, or a speedup claim.
