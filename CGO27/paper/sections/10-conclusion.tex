\section{Conclusion}
Obligation-guided reverification distinguishes three events that ordinary invalidation often conflates: a selected semantic fact becomes invalid, a canonical owner is responsible for re-establishing it, and a particular boundary is the deadline for doing so. Under explicit completeness, routing, scheduling, and verifier-soundness assumptions, P2 fails closed before a due false or undischarged considered fact crosses its first eligible boundary.

The executable demand baseline shows why this is stronger than lazy recomputation: P1D protects a queried invalidated fact, but an otherwise identical unqueried fault can cross the semantic boundary. On the versioned boundary, System W source-to-result, and Language T corpora, P2 and P3 agree on every evaluated classification while P2 avoids routes with no due obligation. The result is a bounded enforcement and localization contribution. It is not a whole-compiler proof, a universal P2/P3 equivalence result, an externally independent evaluation, or a speedup claim.
