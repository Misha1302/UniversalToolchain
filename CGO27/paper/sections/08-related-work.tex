\paragraph{Pass invalidation.}
LLVM and MLIR cache analyses and use explicit preservation to invalidate stale results~\cite{llvmnpm,mlirpm}. This is the strongest material alternative for scheduling work. It answers whether a cached result may be reused and commonly recomputes on demand. Our addition is a verifier-owned obligation whose trigger is a due compiler boundary rather than a subsequent analysis query. The executable demand-driven witness tests this distinction directly.

\paragraph{Translation validation and verified compilation.}
Translation validation checks transformation outputs rather than proving the whole optimizer~\cite{pnueli98,necula00,tristan08}. Alive and Alive2 validate LLVM rewrites~\cite{alive,alive2}; CompCert proves semantic preservation for a compiler stack~\cite{leroy06,leroy09backend}. These approaches provide stronger guarantees within their covered relations. Obligation-guided reverification is weaker and only as sound as its declarations, routes, and verifiers; it targets heterogeneous extension ecosystems where several production checks already exist but need composition-aware scheduling.

\paragraph{Typed, certifying, and testing systems.}
Typed assembly languages, proof-carrying code, and certifying compilers attach checkable evidence to low-level code~\cite{pcc,tal,certcompiler}. Our obligations are transient pipeline duties rather than proofs shipped with binaries. Random and metamorphic systems such as Csmith, EMI, and YARPGen discover wrong-code bugs~\cite{csmith,emi,yarpgen}; reduction systems make failures actionable~\cite{creduce,perses,delta}. We use faults to evaluate localization and detection, not to propose a generator.
