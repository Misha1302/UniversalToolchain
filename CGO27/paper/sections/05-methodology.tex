\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Research questions}
\begin{description}
 \item[RQ1:] Which declared semantic faults are missed by generic structural verification?
 \item[RQ2:] Does the production observer enforce immediate and deferred obligations, and what does deadline-triggered discharge add beyond passive invalidation and demand-driven recomputation?
 \item[RQ3:] How do selective P2 and always P3 compare in detection, localization, and verifier work on the evaluated cases?
 \item[RQ4:] Do the policy distinctions persist in a public-SDK language, and what independent-validity evidence remains unavailable?
\end{description}

\subsection{Versioning and accounting}
Historical P0--P3 records and denominators are immutable; new policies and demand cases use separate versioned catalogues. CI validates both checksums and rejects cross-catalogue rows. Raw JSONL is flushed before validation so failed assertions retain their observations. Repetitions test process isolation and determinism, not independent semantic cases.

\subsection{Implementation-conformance protocol}
Before interpreting corpus outcomes, we test model/code correspondence in the production observer and orchestration contracts. The boundary-order test observes bytecode, AIR, optimized AIR, backend input, and backend compilation in one execution, and establishes that all callbacks receive the same compilation-input identity. A deferred-route test uses one compilation input and one observer instance: an optimized-AIR effect invalidates \texttt{backend.input-verified}; the oracle requires creation at optimized AIR, no semantic backend-route invocation there, carry-forward across the observer return, and discharge immediately before backend compilation. Companion tests cover interleaved compilation identities, pending-obligation carry-forward, overdue rejection, route conflict, owner mismatch, out-of-order callbacks, and cleanup after failure. These are deterministic conformance cases, not independent semantic samples and not part of the 32-program denominator.

\subsection{Units, outcomes, and oracle independence}
The semantic case is the unit of analysis. Predeclared outcomes are classification, diagnostic family, first detection boundary, and source-to-result behavior; policy comparisons use paired discordance counts. Controls remain in every denominator and any rejected control is a false positive.

Oracles are fixed before policy output: declared transitions and deadlines for boundary/ablation cases, specified program results for controls, and mutation-defined diagnostics for faults. The demand pair differs only by a downstream query. P3 is a comparison policy, not an oracle; P2/P3 agreement cannot establish verifier soundness.

\subsection{Boundary corpus}
The historical primary set contains 32 operator shapes, the post-freeze challenge set contains 10, and the historical valid-control set contains 100 controls per policy. Protocol v4 adds P1D, two matched demand cases, and 20 new controls per policy without changing historical denominators. The demand cases differ only in whether a downstream query is issued. The oracle requires P1D to reject the queried case and miss the unqueried case; P2/P3 must reject both at the first eligible boundary.

\subsection{System W source-to-result corpus}
Protocol v3 retains the historical 30 programs as a named study set and adds two matched demand programs. The repaired P07 source and expected value are unchanged. The resulting 32 programs contain 25 valid controls and seven targeted faults. Every case/policy pair runs twice in a fresh process: $32\times5\times2=320$ raw records. The oracle records classification, result, diagnostic family, and first detection boundary. CIL faults can compile to wrong results under non-enforcing policies; interpreter faults can reach a later runtime failure.

\subsection{Language T corpus}
Schema v2 retains 12 historical cases and adds two matched demand cases. The 14 cases comprise two valid programs, two intrinsically invalid programs rejected before post-verification mutation, and ten post-verification faults. Five policies produce 70 observations. P2/P3 classification parity and the P1D queried/unqueried split are hard validation gates.

\subsection{Mechanism ablations}
Eight minimal counterexamples each have a matched valid control. For producer identity, source identity, selected order, canonical ownership, conflict rejection, unresolved routing, backend capability, and repeated occurrence, the full mechanism must emit the predeclared diagnostic and the corresponding one-mechanism ablation must lose that detection without rejecting the control. These are necessity witnesses for the constructed cases, not estimates of real-world prevalence.

\subsection{External and historical validity}
The external-author packet specifies public information, allowed SDK surface, fault/control schema, freeze/checksum procedure, safe import validation, and complete accounting. No human-authored frozen archive was available, so that result is \texttt{BLOCKED\_EXTERNAL}.

For historical evidence, we froze criteria before searching repository issues, fix commits, regression tests, and checked-in review notes through 29 July 2026. The resulting accounting table contains 24 records: three issue-defined semantic defects are included, eleven are excluded by the frozen criteria, and ten aggregate fixes remain blocked because the export lacks an independently derivable one-root reproducer or oracle. We restored the exact selected pre-fix revision offline and ran each included issue case in three fresh processes. All three reproduced in all nine attempts, with no flaky, inconclusive, or infrastructure outcome. This establishes exact pre-fix reproducibility only: the revision predates P2, no policy backport was executed, and we report no historical P2 detection rate.

\subsection{Operational baseline scope}
P3 is the always-verify baseline at every boundary represented by a protocol, using the same routes as P2. It is not identical to a pass manager that invokes a generic verifier after every individual transformation: the latter can create more observation points and may use a different, structural relation. We compare the guarantees and invocation granularity explicitly in related work. The frozen experiments do not provide decision-grade timing for a per-pass verifier, so we do not claim P2 is faster than that configuration.

\subsection{Cost evidence}
Verifier calls and boundary-kernel timing are qualitative work evidence only. Whole-compilation comparisons require a pinned host, controlled load/toolchain, independent process replicates, raw distributions, and paired intervals.
