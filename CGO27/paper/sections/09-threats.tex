\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\paragraph{Authorship and corpus size.}
The primary, challenge, end-to-end, and TensorRules faults are author/model designed. They demonstrate concrete failures but may align with implementation structure. The external blind-corpus claim is blocked until a human-authored archive is frozen before policy execution.

\paragraph{Contract completeness.}
A missing invalidation declaration can prevent P2 from scheduling a needed verifier. P3 is a stronger evaluated oracle only for registered applicable routes; neither policy can detect a property for which no verifier exists. Fail-closed handling of unknown routes reduces silent gaps but does not prove contract completeness.

\paragraph{Generality.}
Two packages are stronger evidence than one, but both execute in one framework and share scheduler concepts. TensorRules intentionally differs in syntax and shape/layout facts, yet it is small and model-authored.

\paragraph{Performance.}
The boundary harness records verifier-kernel timings, and P2 uses fewer calls on clean boundaries. Shared CI is noisy and the reduction is below the frozen threshold. A dedicated capture gate requires a pinned machine, governor, CPU affinity, low background load, 30 process repetitions per cell, and bootstrap intervals. Until that experiment runs, whole-compilation overhead and speedup are unknown.

\paragraph{Baseline product defect.}
P07 exposes a pre-existing mixed-type runtime assertion. Separating it avoids contaminating protocol counts, but also reduces the valid-control set from 25 to 24 in the System W end-to-end study.
