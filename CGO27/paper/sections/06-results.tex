\section{Results}
\subsection{Boundary detection}
Table~\ref{tab:boundary} reports operator-level detection. P0 misses ownership, bytecode metadata, fact-reverification, and most selected-capability relations. P1 records enough contract information to reject many malformed selections but misses four primary operators whose invalidations require semantic discharge. P2 and P3 detect every evaluated primary and challenge operator and reject no valid boundary control.

The paired fixed corpus contains 20 operator shapes detected by P2 but not P0 and four detected by P2 but not P1. P2 and P3 have no discordant evaluated operator. We report these as complete-corpus paired counts rather than as estimates of natural fault prevalence.

\subsection{Invalidation versus demand versus obligation}
The controlled lazy-demand witness creates one invalidated AIR fact. With no consumer demand, lazy scheduling executes zero semantic verifiers and the invalid artifact escapes. Adding an explicit demand schedules one AIR verifier and detects the fault; its matched valid control is accepted. P2 also schedules one verifier and detects the fault without consumer demand. Thus the observed distinction is not ``eager versus lazy execution'' alone: P2 changes the trigger from consumer demand to a due pipeline obligation.

\subsection{Source-to-result behavior}
All five targeted System W faults expose the validity gap. Under CIL, P0/P1 accept compilation and produce result 1 instead of the oracle. Under the interpreter, P0/P1 reach a later runtime/backend exception. P2/P3 reject all targeted cases at the optimized-AIR contract boundary before backend execution. Across all 30 programs, P2/P3 classification, first boundary, diagnostics, and result agree in 30/30 cases. After the numeric-promotion repair, all 25 non-fault programs produce their expected results under all policies.

\subsection{Second language}
In TensorRules, the two valid programs succeed under every policy, and the two intrinsically invalid programs are rejected during initial shape verification. All eight post-verification faults produce wrong results under P0/P1 and are rejected with the expected semantic diagnostic under P2/P3. Selective/always parity is 12/12. On two clean valid boundaries, P2 invokes no semantic verifier and P3 invokes one, confirming distinct schedules through the public SDK.
