\section{Results}
\subsection{Boundary detection}
Table~\ref{tab:boundary} reports operator-level detection. P0 detects structural AIR faults and some target-capability faults but misses ownership, bytecode metadata, and fact-reverification relations. P1 closes most of this gap, but misses four primary operators whose invalidations require semantic discharge. P2 and P3 detect every evaluated primary/challenge operator and reject no valid control.

The paired primary comparison has 20 operators detected by P2 but not P0 (two-sided exact McNemar $p=1.91\times10^{-6}$). P2 detects four operators missed by P1; this small fixed set yields $p=0.125$ and should not be read as a population estimate. P2 and P3 have no discordant evaluated operator.

\subsection{Source-to-result behavior}
All five targeted System W faults expose the validity gap. Under CIL, P0/P1 accept compilation and produce result 1 instead of the expected result. Under the interpreter, P0/P1 reach a later runtime/backend exception. P2/P3 reject all targeted cases at the optimized-AIR contract boundary before backend execution. Across all 30 source programs, P2/P3 classification, first boundary, diagnostics, and result agree in 30/30 cases.

The 24 valid controls produce expected results under all policies. The remaining program, P07, fails identically under all four policies without fault injection; retaining it as a separate baseline runtime failure avoids both hiding a product defect and miscounting it as a protocol false positive.

\subsection{Second language}
In TensorRules, the two valid programs succeed under every policy, and the two intrinsically invalid programs are rejected during initial shape verification. All eight post-verification faults produce wrong results under P0/P1 and are rejected with the expected semantic diagnostic under P2/P3. Selective/always parity is 12/12. On the two clean valid boundaries, P2 invokes no semantic verifier and P3 invokes one, confirming distinct schedules through the public SDK.
