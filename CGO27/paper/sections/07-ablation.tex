\section{Ablation and Verification Work}
We evaluate two complementary levels. Policy-level ablation removes typed contracts (P0) or retains invalidation records without discharge (P1). The demand-driven witness adds a stronger alternative: it discharges an invalidation only after explicit consumer demand. Mechanism-level ablations remove one implementation safeguard at a time.

The full protocol detects all eight counterexamples. Removing each mechanism independently loses the matching detection, while neither the full nor ablated validator rejects the eight controls. These are necessity witnesses for the implementation, not estimates of natural fault frequency or proof of global minimality.

On 100 boundary controls, P2 executes 120 verifier calls and P3 executes 140, a 14.3\% reduction. This supports only the qualitative statement that selective scheduling can avoid unrequested work. It misses the predeclared 25\% threshold and is not whole-compilation timing; therefore efficiency is not a headline claim.
