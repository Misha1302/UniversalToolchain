\section{Obligation-Guided Reverification}
\subsection{Contract construction and ownership}
Each extension contributes immutable facets: owned AST/bytecode identities, compiler-fact ownership, pipeline effects, AIR emissions, backend capabilities, and verifier routes. Selection constructs one table for the actual runtime components. Duplicate owners, unknown facts, impossible effect declarations, and unresolved backend selections are rejected before compilation proceeds.

Ownership is operational, not documentary. It prevents an arbitrary extension from satisfying another component's obligation with a similarly named check. Routes use stable rule identifiers and point to production verifier implementations.

\subsection{Boundary transition}
At each boundary, the scheduler applies the following deterministic transition:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item validate the next component's required facts;
  \item apply generated, preserved, and invalidated fact effects;
  \item create or merge obligations for invalidated verifiable facts;
  \item collect obligations whose due boundary has been reached;
  \item resolve each to exactly one canonical executable route;
  \item execute selected routes in stable rule-ID order; reject on route absence, ownership conflict, verifier failure, or remaining due obligation.
\end{enumerate}
Multiple invalidations mapped to one rule produce one invocation carrying the union of affected facts. This prevents repeated work while preserving fact-level telemetry.

\paragraph{Relative boundary safety.}
Under A1--A4, after a successful transition across boundary $i$, no registered obligation due at or before $i$ remains pending.

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
Proceed by induction over boundaries. The base state has no overdue obligation. At transition $i$, step 3 materializes every newly invalidated registered fact as an obligation. Steps 4--6 either discharge every obligation due by $i$ or reject the transition. Obligations due later remain pending but are not overdue. Therefore a successful transition preserves the invariant. The result says nothing about undeclared invalidations, missing facts, or unsound verifiers.

\subsection{Structural and semantic scopes}
A monolithic IR verifier can make the comparison meaningless by performing semantic checks during mandatory structural validation. We therefore split production verification into non-overlapping scopes. Schema, branch-target, and stack-discipline checks remain structural. Capability, ownership, and selected-contract relations are semantic routes. P3 remains the production default, preserving behavior while P2 is evaluated as a selective alternative.
